# The roadmap

What is left, task by task. Every task says what it is, where it lands,
what proves it is finished, what it needs first, and what it costs. A
task without a proof is a wish.

The numbers are measured, not remembered: the consumer gate reads the
field's repositories as they are today, and the severity register holds
the deviations that are still open. Both are regenerated by the
qualification, so this file is checked against reality rather than
trusted.

**The order of the whole.** The system's own work comes first (A, B),
because a contract that cannot express what the field has decided cannot
judge the field. Then the absorptions, one at a time (C), then the
governance that closes them (D).

**The rule about pushes.** Nothing is pushed to a repository of the field
without the owner's word. A branch is prepared, qualified locally, and
left. A push is a decision, not a step.

## A. The system's own work

### A-1 · A consumer declares its scheme
- **What** The consumer's `gnosix.json` carries the scheme its chrome
  runs on: which roles the scheme decides, and the value of each, per
  substrate. The roles it does not mention keep the system's own values.
- **Lands in** `tools/consumer_check.py` (the declaration's shape and its
  reading) and the consumer's `gnosix.json`.
- **Proved by** the consumer gate runs the declared scheme through
  `tools/audit_scheme.py` and fails when a guarantee breaks.
- **Needs** A-2. The auditor exists; what is missing is the form the
  consumer states it in, and the gate that runs it.
- **Cost** the system: one gate, no run beyond its own qualification.
  The consumer: the declaration, in its own file.

### A-2 · The auditor takes the consumer's scheme, not a file
- **What** `tools/audit_scheme.py` reads a scheme the way a consumer
  writes one — a role, a value, a substrate — and not only the
  contract's own samples. Today it takes a file whose shape the contract
  defines; the consumers write `gnosix.json`, and the two shapes must
  not drift.
- **Lands in** `tools/audit_scheme.py`, and the schema it reads.
- **Proved by** the contract's own two schemes still audit green, and a
  consumer's scheme audits through the same code path.
- **Needs** nothing; it is the shape A-1 hands over.
- **Cost** one gate, no new dependency.

### A-3 · The light substrate of a dynamic scheme
- **What** The field's shell forces the dark scheme, so the light half of
  a dynamic scheme is written and never read. The system keeps its own
  light values there. That is a gap, and it is recorded as one.
- **Lands in** `core/DEVIATIONS.json` (DEV-012, open) and the note in
  `core/schemes.json`.
- **Proved by** the register's probe: the gap is stated and stays stated.
- **Needs** a decision from the field when the light scheme is read.
- **Cost** one entry, no run.

### A-4 · The system states what a scheme may not do
- **What** COL-005 says a scheme that changes the ink changes the ground
  the ink is written on. What a scheme may *not* do is not yet stated:
  the system knows one ground is too light for a light ink, and says so
  only in the samples.
- **Lands in** `core/rules.json`, and the samples in `core/schemes.json`
  that demonstrate it.
- **Proved by** the audit failing on a scheme that breaks the rule, and
  the contract's own samples passing.
- **Needs** nothing.
- **Cost** one rule, no run.

### A-5 · The native profile carries the states — *done*
- **What** The native profile had roles and no states, and the contract's
  elements declare ten. It now names all ten, with the meaning, the role
  and the treatment, and projects them into the crate.
- **Proved by** `tools/project_native_states.py --check`, both ways
  against the contract.

### A-6 · The vocabulary of the field's own spellings
- **What** A consumer may declare that a name of its own is the
  contract's component under another spelling — and may not do it of a
  component the contract does not have.
- **Proved by** `tools/consumer_check.py` and the register's probes.

## B. The discipline

### B-1 · The eye looks at the field's arrangements
- **What** The eye renders the system's own web surfaces and compares the
  pictures with the ones the system carries. It watches the system's
  demo page, not the vocabulary the field writes with. The field's
  thirty-nine names are published by the package and have no specimen.
- **Lands in** `qa/eye/audit.mjs` (the surfaces it renders),
  `qa/eye/baselines/` (the pictures it compares).
- **Proved by** `tools/eye.py` — a surface that moves fails the gate.
- **Needs** the package's sheet rendered in a specimen page.
- **Cost** the system: one gate, no new dependency, the browser is
  cached.

### B-2 · The eye on a native surface
- **What** The eye cannot look at a Rust surface. The field's shell
  renders one; whether it can be rendered headlessly and compared the
  same way is unknown, and the answer decides whether the native
  surfaces are ever watched.
- **Lands in** `qa/eye/` (whatever the answer produces) or
  `core/DEVIATIONS.json` (the answer is no, and it is stated).
- **Proved by** a picture, or by a recorded statement of why there is
  none.
- **Needs** a spike: does the shell's scene renderer run without a
  display?
- **Cost** unknown until the spike is done.

### B-3 · The gates say what they must say — *done*
- **What** `ci/GATES.md` states each gate's expected output and the run
  compares it. Thirty-two gates.

## C. The absorptions, one at a time

### C-0 · The mechanism, shared by all of them
- **What** Every absorption is the same six steps, and they are done on
  a branch, qualified locally, and left for the owner to push.
- **The six steps**
  1. **The branch.** A rehearsal branch, named for what it rehearses.
     The rehearsal stays a rehearsal until the owner says otherwise.
  2. **The declaration.** `gnosix.json`: the version of the system the
     repository honours; the components it keeps as its own, with the
     reason; the components that are the system's under another
     spelling, with the target; the colours that are its content, with
     the reason; the scheme its chrome runs on, when it has one.
  3. **The migration.** The colours of the interface become the
     system's; the colours of the content are declared. The order
     matters: the files that hold the palette first, because everything
     else reads from them.
  4. **The guarantee.** A workflow that clones the system at the
     declared version, beside the repository, and runs the system's own
     gate against it. Not a copy of the gate — a copy goes stale, and a
     stale gate lies.
  5. **The qualification.** The repository's own tests, and the
     consumer gate, locally.
  6. **The push.** On the owner's word.
- **Proved by** the consumer gate green, in the runner, on the commit
  the owner pushed.
- **Cost** one runner run in the consumer's repository, and the owner's
  time to push.

- **Measured** **334** colours outside the contract, **32** components of

- **The steps**

  2. The palette, in the files that hold it: `engine/gnosis-ui/src/theme.rs`

     The static styles become the system's two substrates.

  4. The views follow the theme: the launcher view (40 literals), the
     dock (31), the quick panel (24), the settings chrome (18), the
     omnibar (16), the palette crate (21).
  5. The content colours — the ones the product *shows* — are declared
     with their reason.
  6. The version, the components of its own (the domain vocabulary:
     `omnibar-*`, `quick-*`, `notification-*`, `ui-theme-*`,
     `endpoint-alias`, `urgency`, `slider`), the seven that are the
     system's under another spelling.
  7. The workflow, the qualification, the push.
- **Proved by** the consumer gate green, with the scheme audited.
- **Needs** A-1 and A-2: without the declaration form, the scheme
  cannot be declared, and the dynamic chrome cannot be held to the

- **Cost** one run, on the owner's push. This is the largest of the six.
- **The licence** its root says AGPL-3.0 and forty-seven crates say

## E. What is not in this inventory, and why

- **The consumer's own migrations** are in C; they are the field's work
  and the field's decisions, and the system does them with them.
- **The design's own aesthetic** is not here. The system specifies what
  is allowed and what is legible; it does not choose what a product
  should look like. The field's taste layer does that, and it is
  already written.
- **The shaders** are not here either. `shad.in` and `blob.in` are the
  field's rendering engines; the system's part is the roles, the
  guarantees and the audit, and those are done.
